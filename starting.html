Starting Your Experience

Turn Preparation Into a Strong Partnership

The beginning of an engaged learning experience is where preparation becomes action. Your first conversations with teammates and partners help establish expectations, define responsibilities, and shape how you will work together throughout the project.

Use this stage to create clear communication practices, confirm the project scope, and make sure everyone understands their role before major project work begins.

Why the Beginning Matters

Early decisions can shape the rest of a project. A strong start helps your team move from an abstract project idea to a shared understanding of the work, the people involved, and the expectations everyone is working toward.

Clear roles and communication can also reduce confusion later. Taking time to discuss boundaries, responsibilities, project goals, and communication preferences gives both your team and your partner a stronger foundation for collaboration.

What to Establish at the Start

Focus on these areas as your team begins working with a partner and moves into active project work.

  • Professional communication: Establish a clear way to contact your partner and agree on appropriate communication channels and response expectations.
  • Roles and responsibilities: Clarify what each team member, project lead, instructor, and partner is responsible for during the experience.
  • Shared expectations: Discuss what successful collaboration looks like and make sure everyone understands the project's goals and constraints.
  • Project scope: Confirm the problem your team is addressing, the work that is included, and what may be outside the project's boundaries.
  • Team and project plans: Establish early milestones, responsibilities, meeting practices, and ways to track progress.
  • Partnership agreements: Document important commitments so expectations are visible and shared by everyone involved.

Prepare for Your First Partner Meeting

Your first meeting is an opportunity to learn more about the partner, clarify the project, and begin establishing a professional working relationship.

Before the Meeting

  • Review what your team already knows about the organization and project.
  • Identify the most important questions that are still unanswered.
  • Decide who will lead different parts of the meeting.
  • Prepare an agenda and share it with the partner when appropriate.
  • Confirm the meeting time, location or virtual platform, and expected participants.

During the Meeting

  • Introduce team members and clarify everyone's role.
  • Ask the partner to describe their organization, needs, priorities, and goals in their own words.
  • Discuss the intended users or communities connected to the project.
  • Clarify project expectations, constraints, deadlines, and desired outcomes.
  • Agree on communication methods and how often the team and partner should connect.
  • Record important decisions, questions, and next steps.

After the Meeting

  1. Review your notes with the team.
  2. Document decisions, responsibilities, and open questions.
  3. Send a follow-up message summarizing important next steps when appropriate.
  4. Update your project plan or scope based on what you learned.

Create a Communication Plan

A communication plan helps your team and partner know where information should be shared, when updates are expected, and who is responsible for communicating important decisions.

Agree on the following before the project becomes busy:

  • Which communication channels will be used for different types of messages?
  • Who is the main contact for the student team?
  • Who is the main contact for the partner organization?
  • How often will the team provide progress updates?
  • How quickly should team members and partners normally respond to messages?
  • How will meetings, decisions, and project changes be documented?
  • What should happen if an urgent issue or project concern arises?

Questions to Confirm With Your Partner

Early conversations should help both sides develop a shared understanding of the project. These questions can help uncover assumptions before they become problems.

  • What problem or need is most important to the partner?
  • Who are the people or communities affected by the work?
  • What does a successful outcome look like to the partner?
  • What work or research has already been completed related to this project?
  • Are there important limitations, policies, deadlines, or technical constraints the team should know about?
  • What information, people, or resources can the partner provide to support the work?
  • Who has authority to approve important project decisions?
  • How and when would the partner like to review work and provide feedback?

Ready to Move Into Active Project Work?

Once roles, expectations, project scope, and communication practices are established, your team is ready to focus on managing the work, communicating progress, gathering feedback, and responding to challenges as the project develops.
