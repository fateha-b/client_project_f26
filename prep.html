Preparing for Your Experience

Build a Strong Foundation Before the Work Begins

Engaged learning asks you to apply your knowledge in real-world situations with organizations, communities, teammates, and other partners. Before beginning the work, take time to understand the context of the experience, reflect on your own perspective, and determine what your team needs in order to contribute effectively.

Thoughtful preparation can help your team enter the experience with clearer expectations, stronger awareness, and a better understanding of the people and communities connected to the work.

Why Preparation Matters

Engaged learning is different from completing a traditional classroom assignment. The project may involve real organizational needs, community priorities, changing constraints, and people who will be affected by the work.

Before proposing solutions, it is important to listen, ask questions, and understand the situation you are entering. Reflecting on your assumptions and learning about the project's context can help you approach the experience as a collaborative learner rather than assuming you already know the best solution.

What to Focus on Before You Begin

Use these priorities to identify what you and your team should understand before moving into active project work.

  • Set learning goals: Identify the technical, professional, interpersonal, and ethical skills you hope to develop during the experience.
  • Understand expectations: Review project requirements, commitments, responsibilities, deadlines, and expected outcomes.
  • Develop social and community awareness: Consider your own identity, experiences, assumptions, and positionality while learning about the history and context of the community or organization.
  • Assess team readiness: Identify team members' skills, interests, availability, responsibilities, and areas where additional knowledge or support may be needed.
  • Understand the project context: Learn about the organization, intended users, affected stakeholders, constraints, and existing work related to the project.
  • Begin project scoping: Identify the partner's needs and desired outcomes without committing to a solution before you understand the problem.

Questions to Consider

Discuss these questions individually and with your team before beginning the project.

  • What do I hope to learn from this experience?
  • What experiences or assumptions may influence how I understand this project?
  • Who will be affected by the project and its outcomes?
  • What do we already know about the organization or community?
  • What knowledge and skills does our team already have?
  • What knowledge, skills, or perspectives may be missing from our team?
  • What do we still need to learn from the partner?
  • How will the partner or community participate in project decisions?
  • What would a mutually beneficial outcome look like?

A Preparation Process

Work through these steps before your team begins defining solutions or final deliverables.

  1. Reflect on Your Perspective

    Consider the experiences, identities, assumptions, and areas of privilege that may influence how you approach the project.

  2. Learn About the Context

    Research the organization, community, project history, affected stakeholders, and any broader conditions that may shape the work.

  3. Set Learning Goals

    Identify what you want to learn or strengthen through the experience, including technical and professional skills.

  4. Review Team Capacity

    Compare your team's skills, knowledge, responsibilities, and availability with the likely needs of the project.

  5. Identify Open Questions

    Write down what your team still needs to learn from the partner before confirming the project's scope, approach, or deliverables.

Featured Preparation Resources

These tools can help you reflect, understand your project context, clarify responsibilities, and begin planning your work.

  • Social Identity Snapshot : A tool for reflecting on identity and positionality before entering community or project contexts.
  • "Check Yourself" Community Engagement Checklist : A checklist for examining assumptions and preparing for thoughtful community engagement.
  • IAP2 Spectrum of Public Participation : A framework for understanding different levels of public participation and decision-making power.
  • Project Scoping Handout & Terms : Guidance for translating partner needs into realistic project goals.
  • Student Organization Officer Guide and Project Manager Guide : Guidance on roles, expectations, and commitments for student leaders.
  • Project Manager Commitment Form : An agreement outlining responsibilities for student project leads.
  • Project Management Worksheet : A worksheet for clarifying project scope, objectives, team roles, and timelines with the project team and client.

Ready to Begin?
